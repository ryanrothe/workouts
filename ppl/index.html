How this tracker works

Six workouts, one per training day: Push A → Pull A → Legs A → Push B → Pull B → Legs B. Tap a tab to open that day. Inside it, hit + New to start a dated session, check exercises off as you finish them, and tap ✎ Log to record the weight you used.

When you log, enter the weight and tap ✓ Hit or ✗ Missed — did every set land inside the prescribed rep range? That one tap is what drives the weight suggestions. Tap it again to clear it.

Next time you do that workout, the app does the progression math for you and shows a suggested load. Tap the suggestion to fill it in:

  • Green — "Try 190 lb": you hit the range last time, so it adds 5 lb.
  • Grey — "Hold 185 lb, beat your reps": you missed, so the weight stays put. Per the program, you don't drop it.
  • Amber — "Plateau: drop to 165 lb × 8-10": three logged sessions at the same weight with no successful one. It cuts ~10% and adds 2 reps to the range automatically.

Suggestions only appear once there's a logged weight to work from, so bodyweight movements stay quiet until you start adding load to them. The Up Next stat tells you which day is due based on what you trained most recently — it rotates through the six, so a missed day doesn't throw off the cycle.

Garage substitutions

Cable and machine lifts carry a ⇄ Sub button with 2-3 garage-friendly swaps — bands, dumbbells, kettlebells, dip bar. Rules:

  • A swap sticks. Once you sub a lift, every new session starts on that sub so training stays consistent week over week. Switching back to the program's lift (or another sub) is one tap in ⇄ Sub, any time.
  • Each variation keeps its own history. "Last:" and the weight suggestions only look at sessions where you did that same exercise — dips numbers never feed cable-stack suggestions.
  • Sets × reps stay as prescribed for whatever variation you're on.
  • Made the swap mid-workout without tapping it? Open the session, tap ⇄ Sub, pick what you actually did — the logged weight follows the choice.

Bodyweight lifts and added weight

Dips, pull-ups, calf raises, and hanging leg raises log added weight, not total weight — the field reads BW +. Three states:

  • Blank: untracked. No history, no suggestions.
  • 0: strict bodyweight, tracked. Hit the range at BW and the coach suggests "Try BW +5 lb" — time for the belt and a kettlebell.
  • A number: pounds clipped on. Progression math runs on the added weight, same rules as the barbell lifts, and a plateau can drop you back to strict BW.

Rest timers are pre-set per exercise: 2 min on squats and deadlifts, 90s on the main lifts, 60s on accessories.

The split

  • Push A (Mon) — barbell, heavy. Low reps on the presses.
  • Pull A (Tue) — vertical + horizontal pulling, calves, laterals.
  • Legs A (Wed) — squat-led, plus cable arm work.
  • Push B (Thu) — dumbbell, higher rep. Same muscles, different stimulus.
  • Pull B (Fri) — machine and cable rows, plus single-arm arm work.
  • Legs B (Sat) — deadlift-led, unilateral work, core, traps.

Progression model

Reverse linear periodization. In plain terms:

  • Start each lift at a weight you're confident you can handle for the reps listed.
  • Hit every set inside the rep range? Add the smallest increment available next time you do that workout — roughly 5 lb on the main lifts.
  • Missed the reps? Don't drop the weight. Log what you did and try to beat it next time. Once every set lands in range, add weight again.
  • Plateaued (2 to 3 weeks with no rep or weight increase)? Cut the weight about 10% and raise the rep range by 2 — a 6-8 becomes an 8-10. Volume keeps climbing, your joints get a break from constant weight PRs.
  • Main lifts (bench, squat, deadlift, pulldowns) climb regularly. Accessories (curls, triceps, raises) often won't — that's expected. Just do a little better each time.

Execution standards

  • Perfect form, no body English. Hit parallel on squats, touch the chest on bench.
  • Keep tension on the muscle through the whole set — this is a mind-muscle-connection program, not a move-the-weight program.
  • No machine? Substitute. Pulldowns for pull-ups, DB rows for the row machine — the pattern matters more than the equipment.

Block length

Designed as a 3-month block, but the exercise variety supports running it longer. Log honestly and the "last load" reference will tell you when you've genuinely stalled versus just had a bad day.

Nutrition, per the program

  • Fat loss: 1 g protein per pound of goal bodyweight; calories at roughly bodyweight × 10. Adjust if the scale isn't moving week to week.
  • Muscle gain: same protein target; calories at roughly bodyweight × 14-16. Adjust if weight isn't climbing.
